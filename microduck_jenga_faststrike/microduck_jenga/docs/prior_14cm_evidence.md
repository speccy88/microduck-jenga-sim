# Prior 14 cm Jenga demonstration

The later 2026-10-29 local experiment is separate from the three-block PPO experiment in `local_rtx_training.md`. The saved demonstration shows the upper of two vertical, free-body blocks falling while the lower block and robot remain standing. The archived video `microduck_14cm_gen_100_best_evaluated_seed_43000.mp4` hashes to `cd5109930c5d6c73addd95a5e10fca3641bcf7e11885e3b26eed73985557bc0d` and its saved generation-100 parameter checkpoint hashes to `796d2e68f44e4ce10f1ed3a8666fe158b7abd8e2f22f908eac5045d49eae6234`.

The preserved training source optimizes head-position and timing parameters with the cross-entropy method (CEM), commands head joints using those parameters and uses a frozen pretrained VelStand neural network for balance. The controller uses ideal simulator geometry for bearing. It is a learned parameterized trajectory/controller, not an end-to-end PPO policy or a camera/ToF policy. The setup starts with the two-block tower 14 cm from the robot; the new Vertical3 task starts with three blocks 20 cm away. Both are simulation-only.

The archived report records 8/8 successes in the selection batch and 13/16 in a separate held-out batch for generation 100; the top fell in all 16 held-out episodes, with one lower-block failure and no reported robot falls or forbidden-contact episodes. The held-out batch used one batch seed and 16 parallel episode indices, rather than 16 independently chosen batch seeds. Its recorded Wilson interval is approximately 57.0%–93.4%. The report states that the original success thresholds were retained and contact/strike data were explicitly refreshed at each physics substep; the historical substep implementation and its freshness must be independently validated before using that statement as proof of measured impact accuracy. The 20 cm transfer probe reported 0/8 successes.

The historical checkpoint was replayed locally on 30 new reset seeds 61000–61029: 25/30 satisfied the recorded criteria, or 83.3% (95% Wilson interval 66.4%–92.7%). The top fell and head contact was recorded in all 30; no robot falls, lower-block failures or forbidden contacts were recorded. The five remaining episodes failed the fast-strike criterion. Mean lower-block maximum displacement was 1.24 mm, maximum 7.64 mm; mean maximum tilt was 1.00 degrees, maximum 3.22 degrees. Mean first-contact forward head speed was 0.279 m/s; mean successful separation delay was 0.167 s. Environment startup randomization used the fixed seed 41; the 30 seeds independently vary episode resets, so this is not a broad startup-domain-randomization study. No physical robot test was performed.

On a separately repeated successful episode, a numerical check compared CPU MuJoCo contacts and Warp sensor contacts at matching positions for 201 physics substeps around impact. Both reported head/top contact on the same 12 substeps; maximum mouth-position disagreement was approximately 5.1e-8 m. A repeated failed episode near the speed threshold also matched on all 201 checked substeps, with eight contact substeps and maximum mouth-position disagreement approximately 6.5e-8 m. These checks support the contact instrumentation for those sampled cases, not every possible impact, the complete velocity calculation or physical hardware accuracy. Do not redistribute pretrained weights before confirming the applicable distribution rights.

The separately shared `MicroDuck_Jenga_scripted_local_attempt.mp4` is a different three-block preview. Its SHA-256 `1f1f7e479efa67d34f1a2d53dfb2f6c326bdaada120b46ac853e1991ca9573ee` matches the archived `scripted_close_head_push.mp4`. Its simulation sidecar records the tower at 10 cm, first head contact at 0.945 s, 267 contact substeps, no wrong contacts or robot fall, and a stable base. The upper block did not fall; final horizontal displacement was approximately 1.0 mm. The script uses timed approach/head/withdraw commands with pretrained VelStand for balance, and explicitly records `training_performed: false`. That video is a real simulated contact demonstration, not a learned Jenga policy or task success.

The original CEM replay uses a modified `mdp.py` that skips the ordinary 50 Hz strike update while the high-rate monitor is active. This publication candidate restores an equivalent guard in its shared task source. The separate development PPO branch lacks that guard, so attaching the historical monitor there without first restoring it would update the strike state twice and invalidate the measurement.

Source inspection of mjlab 1.3.0 shows that each physics substep calls `scene.update()` before `metrics_manager.compute_substep()`, and the sensor base class invalidates its cache in `update()`. The historical monitor then calls `sim.forward()` and `sim.sense()` before reading contacts. This ordering is consistent with its intended refresh. The two numerical CPU/GPU contact comparisons described above provide limited checks at matched simulated positions.
